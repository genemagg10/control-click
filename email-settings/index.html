Email Settings for Every Major Provider

A mail app just asked you for an "incoming server" and a port number, and you'd rather be doing anything else. Here are the IMAP, POP, and SMTP settings for the major providers, plus plain-English answers to what those words mean.

Why this page exists: one of the first things Gene ever built for the web was an email setup page just like this one. Hundreds of thousands of people found it right when they were stuck, and it just… helped. Most mail apps configure themselves now — but if you've landed here mid-setup with a blinking cursor in a "server" field, this page is for you. Some traditions are worth keeping.

Thirty seconds of orientation

IMAP — receiving (recommended)

Your mail stays on the server, so your phone, laptop, and webmail all show the same inbox. If your app offers IMAP or POP, choose IMAP.

POP — receiving (the old way)

Downloads mail to one device and usually removes it from the server. Only use it if you have a specific reason (or a very old app).

SMTP — sending

The outgoing side. If you can read mail but not send it — or send but not receive — one of the two halves is misconfigured, and now you know which.

Ports & SSL/TLS

The port is the numbered door the connection uses; SSL/TLS means it's encrypted. Use the settings exactly as listed — "SSL/TLS" and port 993/995/465, or "STARTTLS" with 587. Never pick "none."

One more thing before the tables: your username is almost always your full email address, and several providers (Google, Apple, Yahoo, AT&T) won't accept your normal password in a classic mail app — they issue a special app password instead. If your correct password keeps getting rejected, that's nearly always the reason.

If it still won't connect

  • Retype, don't trust. The classic culprits: a space at the end of the address, autocorrect capitalizing your username, or an old saved password.
  • Check the password type. Google, Apple, Yahoo, and AT&T all want an app password or mail key in classic apps — your regular password will be rejected forever, correctly typed or not.
  • Match port to security. 993/995/465 pair with SSL/TLS; 587 pairs with STARTTLS. A right server on a wrong port fails in confusing ways.
  • Sending broken, receiving fine? That's SMTP. Confirm the outgoing server requires authentication (use the same username and password as incoming).
  • On a company or custom domain? Your settings come from whoever hosts your email, not this page — check with them, or ask us to untangle it.

Settings last reviewed August 2026. Providers do occasionally change these — if something here seems off, check your provider's official support page, and tell us so we can fix it for the next person.
